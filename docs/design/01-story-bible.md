# VARDHOLM
## Canonical Story Bible — v1.0 (Production Canon)
**Tagline:** SURVIVE THE ISLAND. DISCOVER THE TRUTH.

> This document supersedes Pitches A, B and C. Where they conflict, this wins. Everything downstream — level design, puzzle specs, audio, localisation, marketing — cites this file.

---

## 1. LOGLINE + PREMISE

A disgraced marine acoustician, shipwrecked on an island that three centuries of charts insist is a phantom shoal, discovers that a Bronze Age culture carved the entire mountain into a machine that keeps the sea quiet — that a 1970s research programme broke it trying to copy it, and that one woman has been holding the damage shut, alone, for thirty-one years.

**Premise.** Survival is the first hour, not the game. The signature verb is **listening**: the player carries a hydrophone and field recorder and learns to read the island as a resonant body. The island is inert, ancient and entirely physical — the horror is institutional (a programme that costed the harm and proceeded) and domestic (a 66-year-old woman doing unpaid maintenance because nobody ever released her from a contract with a dead employer).

## 2. THE ISLAND — **VARDHOLM**

*(Local survey designation on the falsified 1961 chart: "Pellamar Shoal.")*

An eroded stratovolcanic caldera in the deep South Atlantic, ~11 km across, rim standing 240 m out of the water on three sides, ringed by a semi-permanent orographic cloud lid.

**Why it is not on charts — two causes, one history.** The caldera's iron-rich rim scatters radar into something that reads as weather, and the cloud lid defeats optical satellite passes. That got it logged as a doubtful vigia. Then in **1961** a hydrographic vessel under contract to the Ferrier–Oleander Consortium filed a **deliberately false depth sounding** so the site would stay private. Every later chart inherited the lie. There is no anomaly in the compasses and no unexplained physics: the island is missing because of atmosphere, geology and one act of paperwork fraud nobody audited.

**Four occupation layers:**

| Layer | Dates | Who | What they left |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I — The Tolo Vardh** | c. 2100–1380 BCE | Ocean-going settlers on an island with rain but no aquifer. Cut the mountain into condensation and damper architecture over six centuries. | Tool marks, wear patterns, a notch-and-crescent tally notation, six generational maintenance registers. Non-verbal. No mysticism. |
| **II — The Cormorant Trust** | 1883–1891 | British phosphate venture. Misread the terraces as burial works and quarried a third of them out. | Rev. Silas Poulter's journal — the document that, reprinted in 1969, brought everyone else here. |
| **III — Ferrier–Oleander Consortium / Project Wellhead** | 1961–1976 | Private infrasound programme, then a Dutch-Brazilian hydrological consortium that came to reverse-engineer the ancient system. Built Substation Oleander and Fold Camp. | Journals, reel-to-reel logs, load calculations, contact sheets, variance reports. |
| **IV — The Orrimond Trust and the tender** | 1976–1994, then 1994–present | A liability-holding foundation that staffed the site with rotating caretakers, dissolved in 1994 when its endowment was absorbed by a bank that never read the file. One caretaker never left. | Brass maintenance tags, chalked tide tables, 11,000+ logbook entries, a wall of unmailed letters. |

**The nine zones — one sensory identity each:**

1. **The Ribcage** (shore) — black sand studded with six wrecked hulls set too evenly to be accident; they were towed into line as a breakwater.
2. **Fernmaw** (jungle) — tree-fern canopy grown inside a collapsed stone aqueduct trench, so you walk a graded channel with green closing overhead; the floor is machete-cut and raked.
3. **Fold Camp** (abandoned camp) — orange fibreglass huts on stilts over a tidal flat, laundry bleached bone-white on the line, boots paired at every door.
4. **The Combs** (ruins) — terraced basalt honeycomb in a cliff face; a beehive from below, a circuit board from above, with a modern steel ladder bolted straight through the carvings.
5. **The Sweatways** (caves) — retrofitted lava tubes with cut-stone baffles and ceramic drip-combs; warm, loud, wet, and the air pressure shifts audibly on an eleven-minute cycle.
6. **Rime Shoulder** (mountain) — above the cloud lid: cold, blinding, silent, the only place on Vardholm you can see sky, with rope bridges re-roped in new hemp over old steel cable.
7. **Ash Throat** (volcano) — a dormant sulphur-yellow vent bowl, venting not erupting, with copper pipe run into it by someone who needed heat.
8. **Substation Oleander** (underground facility) — 1960s poured concrete, brass fittings and phenolic dials meeting dressed Bronze Age stone in a visible seam. Clean. Lit. Humming.
9. **The Quiet Room** (final area) — a circular chamber at the caldera's acoustic focus, floor covered in thirty-one years of chalk, ceiling carved into inverted fins that are still wet after four thousand years.

## 3. THE PROTAGONIST — **NADIA VESK**, 41

**Discipline.** Marine acoustician; formerly lead analyst on a long-baseline hydrophone array.

**Backstory.** Eighteen months ago she logged an anomalous coherent low-frequency event in her data. Under funder pressure for a clean quarterly, she reclassified it as instrument noise and deleted the raw capture. Six weeks later a survey vessel was lost in that same water with nine aboard. The inquiry cleared her on the record and ended her professionally. She now does sonar calibration for a cable-laying contractor, which is beneath her, and she knows it.

**Why she is here.** She chased the signal again, off-book, on a chartered ketch with one skipper, Tomas Weir, who does not surface after the reef takes them in the prologue.

**Voice.** Methodical, self-interrogating, allergic to her own certainty. She narrates the way analysts do: observation, then the caveat. "Two-forty hertz, steady. Or my recorder's dying. Check it twice before you write it down."

**Starting equipment (canonical Act 1 loadout).** A **dry bag** containing a battered directional **hydrophone**, a solid-state **field recorder** with 9% battery, a multitool, a pencil, and the **Field Slate** — her wire-bound notebook, which becomes the game's evidence system: entries can be logged, cross-referenced, and *revised*.

**Why she cannot wait for rescue.** Three interlocking reasons, all established in Act 1: nobody knows where she is, because the trip was off-book and unfiled; the island is charted as a shoal, so no search grid will include it; and her ketch's EPIRB went down with the hull. Waiting is a decision to die anonymously with an incomplete record — the exact shape of the failure that ruined her. Her engine is not survival. It is **completion of the record**.

## 4. THE CAST OF ABSENT PEOPLE

**Rev. Silas Poulter** — chaplain-surveyor, Cormorant Trust, 1883–1891. Cramped copperplate, scripture, moral self-justification, and gorgeous accidental observation he cannot interpret. *Sample:* "The rock sweats at the cold hour, as though pitying us; I have forbidden the men to speak of it as a miracle."
**The player learns:** the terraces were functional, not funerary — and his Trust is why a third of them no longer work.

**Dr. Ivo Ferrier** — founder, Ferrier–Oleander Consortium, 1961–1969. Fountain pen, imperial units, writes in the collective *we* even when alone. *Sample:* "The bowl is listening. We need only stop interrupting it."
**The player learns:** the public science (long-range infrasound detection) and the private ambition (sell early-warning to governments) — and that he never understood what he was standing inside.

**Marguerite Sabo** — instrument engineer, 1964–1976. Reel-to-reel logs; dry, profane, numbers everything, treats Ferrier as a hazard to be routed around. *Sample:* "Log fourteen. Ferrier's 'anomaly' is a loose gland nut on Line Three. I have now fixed it twice."
**The player learns:** the truth of what Oleander does, because she built it — the damper gates hidden inside Ferrier's grand listening post.

**Dr. Margit Solheim** — principal investigator, Project Wellhead, 1971–1976. Precise, funny, counting aloud, and unable to revise a conclusion. *Sample:* "Day one hundred four. Yield down nine percent. I am not going to write the word *sabotage* again."
**The player learns:** the mechanism of the damage. She bored new apertures into the ancient chambers to force output, destabilised the balance, and kept going because being wrong in public had already cost her once.

**Hanne Lorvik** — third-term Orrimond caretaker, arrived 1994 aged 29, now 66. **Alive. Present. On the island.** Her medium is not journal but *work*: dated brass tags, inventory cards, chalked tide tables, 11,000+ logbook entries in handwriting that degrades across decades while the wording never changes. *Sample:* "Day 11,204. Lantern 7 replaced. Bridge 2 re-roped. No vessel."
**The player learns:** everything, last — and only in person.

## 5. THE SECRET — THE SINGLE CANONICAL ANSWER

**Vardholm is not an island with ruins on it. Vardholm is a machine, and the ruins are its working parts — and its purpose is subtraction, not production.**

The caldera is a natural acoustic lens. Under certain tidal and thermal states it re-radiates ambient ocean infrasound as a coherent, focused low-frequency beam down the deep sound channel — loud enough at range to wreck the navigation and the biology of anything in that corridor. The **Tolo Vardh** spent six centuries carving the mountain to detune it: fin-cut condensation halls, thermal chimneys, cut-stone baffles and gravity-fed water ballast that together damp the resonance and, as a by-product, condense fresh water on an island with no aquifer. Water was how they *paid* for the work. Quiet was the work. Their society was organised around maintenance, and the notch-and-crescent mark is a title — **Weir-Keeper** — held by six generations, not one person.

**Ferrier** found the acoustic effect and misread it as reception. **Sabo** worked out it was emission, and built Substation Oleander's damper gates as a modern prosthesis for the failing ancient ones. **Project Wellhead** came for the water design and could not abstract it — the geometry is locked to Vardholm's specific thermal gradient — and Solheim's forced modifications broke the balance in both functions at once: the Combs dried, and the caldera got louder. Wellhead did not die of monsters. They argued, they thirsted, and the last of them sealed the Sump to stop the others making it worse. The Orrimond Trust inherited the liability, hired caretakers, then ceased to exist. **Nobody ever told Hanne Lorvik she could stop.** It needs roughly forty hours of physical labour a month, forever, by a human on site. There is no automation that survives salt air.

**Belief ladder:**
- **Hour 1:** *"I'm shipwrecked on a rock that isn't on the chart. Water, shelter, signal."*
- **Hour 3:** *"Someone was here before me, twice over — a 1970s camp, and something four thousand years older under it. And this jungle path was cut last week."*
- **Hour 7:** *"The ancients built a machine to keep the ocean quiet. The researchers broke it. One woman has been holding it shut alone for thirty-one years — and the signal I deleted was this island getting loud."*

## 6. THE FIVE ACTS

**ACT 1 — SURVIVAL** *(The Ribcage, Fernmaw)*
**Goal:** Fresh water inside 36 in-game hours, and dry out the field recorder before the batteries corrode.
**New capability:** Core crafting and item combination — cordage, cutting edge, fire, the dry box; and the **Catchment**, a crude one-litre condensation rig. Thesis mechanic introduced as a survival tool before the player knows it is the theme.
**Revelation:** The gully that saved her has cut stone under the moss, graded at a constant 1.5 degrees — and the machete cuts on the vines above it are days old, not decades.
**Open question:** Who grades a hillside, and who is still cutting the path?

**ACT 2 — THE SIGNAL** *(Fernmaw, The Sweatways, Rime Shoulder)*
**Goal:** Get above the cloud lid to transmit or be seen, and find the source of the eleven-minute pressure pulse.
**New capability:** **The hydrophone** becomes the signature verb — aim it and the world resolves into a directional spectrogram overlay revealing water behind rock, machinery behind walls, and the caldera's resonance nodes. All puzzles from here are acoustic. Plus climbing and the **Field Slate** evidence system.
**Revelation:** Sabo's reels in a ridge relay hut: contempt for Ferrier's public story, and the first mention of "the gates." From the summit, the fumarole arc in Ash Throat resolves into a deliberate curve, and the 1970s mast is already down and already stripped.
**Open question:** Someone else already tried to signal out. Why didn't it work?

**ACT 3 — THE CAMP** *(Fold Camp, Ash Throat)*
**Goal:** Restore power, read Wellhead's records, understand the rotation system.
**New capability:** Electrical repair, the **Reel Deck** (carryable playback, so a 1974 voice narrates the room you stand in), and the **maintenance ledger** — every serviced object wears a dated brass tag, turning housekeeping into navigation. Follow the freshest tags.
**Revelation:** Wellhead were copyists, not surveyors: Castellar's load calculations reference structures 3,900 years older than the camp. Then a grave, dug and marked and *maintained*, with fresh shore flowers — and a radio dead since 1989 with new batteries in it.
**Open question:** The Combs ran wet in 1972 and are dry now. What changed, and who is keeping this place alive?

**ACT 4 — THE RUINS** *(The Combs, Substation Oleander)*
**Goal:** Learn to read Tolo Vardh notation, restart one cell, and get inside Oleander.
**New capability:** **The Register** (carvings convert into legible flow and damping schematics) and **the damper console** — ballast, vent and tension across four gates, tuned live against a tide table. The player learns to *do the job*.
**Revelation:** Weir-Keeper is a title across six generations. Poulter quarried a third of the system; Solheim bored into the rest. And the Orrimond Trust's 1994 dissolution letter sits unopened in the mail tray beside 11,000 logbook entries.
**Open question:** She read the envelope and never opened it. Why?

**ACT 5 — THE TRUTH** *(The Quiet Room)*
**Goal:** Meet Hanne Lorvik. Decide what happens to Vardholm.
**New capability:** **None. The crafting HUD is stripped entirely.** The only verbs are walk, listen and speak. Protect this in design review.
**Revelation:** Lorvik has been avoiding Nadia for eleven days — not hostility, but thirty-one years of rehearsing a conversation she cannot start. The chalk floor is every ship she has heard pass, uncontacted, since 1994.
**Open question:** Answered. Then the cost is presented.

## 7. THE ENDING

Nadia has a working transmitter and a fixed position. Lorvik has a failing body, no successor, and a machine that needs forty hours a month forever.

- **THE RECORD.** Transmit everything — position, Oleander's schematics, the Tolo Vardh register, the full unedited log. Rescue comes. Lorvik leaves. Vardholm gets surveyed, argued over, and probably mismanaged; the gates sit untended for the eighteen months it takes institutions to agree on anything, and the channel gets loud. Nadia finally refuses to simplify a truth, and other people pay for the interval. Lorvik, on the boat, is not grateful — she has no idea who she is off this island.
- **THE RELIEF.** Transmit nothing. Put Lorvik on the outbound supply drift with the complete record sealed in the dry box, and stay. Take the tags. Take the chalk. The last shot is Nadia writing *Day 1* in a new book, in handwriting that already looks a little like Lorvik's. The record survives without her; she does not.

**The cost, stated:** The Record buys Nadia's absolution with other people's safety. The Relief buys other people's safety with Nadia's absolution — and it is the ending where she finally stops editing the data, because she is no longer in it. There is no third option, no automation cheat, no epilogue crawl saying which was right. Both endings close on the Quiet Room ceiling, still dripping: in **Record** the drips falter out of rhythm; in **Relief** they settle into one.

## 8. RULES OF THE FICTION — THE ANTI-INCONSISTENCY CONTRACT

1. **Nothing supernatural is ever confirmed, hinted at, or left ambiguous as a tease.** Every effect on Vardholm has a stated physical cause: acoustics, thermodynamics, geology, corrosion. No consciousness, no entity, no "the island wants."
2. **The island never acts. People act.** Vardholm has no agency, no malice and no favourites. Harm on this island is always traceable to a human decision with a date and a signature.
3. **No creature threat, no combat, no weapons, ever.** No hostile fauna, no stalker, no chase sequence. Environmental pressure is corrosion, humidity, cold, thirst and falls. The signature tool is a microphone.
4. **The Tolo Vardh are competent, not mystical.** No prophecy, no ritual, no chosen ones, no "lost wisdom." They were engineers who organised a society around upkeep. Never depict them as primitive or as magical.
5. **Technology behaves its age.** 1960s gear is electromechanical, serviceable, and fails at gaskets, relays and brushes. Nothing built before 1994 is networked, remotely operable, or self-repairing. Batteries corrode, tape stretches, salt eats everything.
6. **Exactly one living human besides Nadia is on the island.** Hanne Lorvik. She is never a threat, never violent, and never seen clearly before Act 5 — only her *work* is seen. No second survivor, no reveal that she is imagined.
7. **The record is never destroyed by the plot.** The Field Slate can be wrong and can be revised, but the narrative never wipes it as a cheap escalation. Revision is the theme; erasure is the sin.
8. **Compasses, GPS and clocks work normally.** The island is invisible because of cloud, radar scatter and a 1961 falsified sounding — not because instruments fail near it. Never write a spinning-needle beat.
9. **The caldera's loud state is a slow, physical, off-screen consequence.** It is never a real-time hazard that damages the player, and it never manifests as a visible beam or an on-screen monster.
10. **All science in shipped text is dramatised, not claimed.** No marketing or in-game document asserts real-world accuracy. Treat the acoustic-lens and passive-condensation premises as invented fiction requiring an acoustics and a hydrology consultant review before any realism claim is made publicly.

## 9. ORIGINALITY STATEMENT

Nearest comparables, and the concrete separation:

- **Myst / Riven.** Shares only the unprotectable skeleton of a deserted engineered place read through documents. Those are *abandonment* mysteries resolved by authored magic and family melodrama. Vardholm is *currently serviced*; its mechanisms have stated physical operating principles; and the pressure in the room is an exhausted 66-year-old doing unpaid maintenance. No shared names, locations, puzzles or plot beats.
- **Subnautica.** Shares ocean setting and a crafting loop. Differs absolutely: nothing non-human appears at any point, there is no combat, no ecology threat, and the "alien-scale" layer is a Bronze Age human culture. Signature tool is a hydrophone, not a weapon.
- **Return to Mysterious Island (Kheops) / Verne.** Shares only shipwreck-plus-inventory-combination, an unprotectable genre convention. No Verne characters, locations, submarine, hidden benefactor, companion animal or inherited technology appears here, and none may be added. Every proper noun, character, document, zone and puzzle grammar in this bible is newly invented for this project.

**The load-bearing originality:** the mystery is a *maintenance problem*. The ancients were competent rather than wise; the antagonist is a good scientist who could not revise a conclusion; and the final antagonistic pressure is an employment contract with a dead foundation.

**Unverified assumption flagged for clearance:** "VARDHOLM", "Pellamar", "Tolo Vardh", "Substation Oleander", "Orrimond Trust", "Ferrier–Oleander Consortium", "Project Wellhead", "Cormorant Trust" and all character names have **not** been trademark- or title-searched by me. Run a full clearance pass before any public use.
