# VARDHOLM — World Structure Document

## Lead Level Design · v1.0 · Cites Story Bible v1.0

---

## 0. STRUCTURAL THESIS

**Vardholm is a hub-and-spoke caldera read in three passes.**

The world is a **ring, not a line**. Fold Camp sits at the geometric centre of the playable island — a tidal flat inside the caldera's south breach — and every zone is a spoke off it. The player reaches Fold Camp at the end of Act 1 and it never stops being home: it holds the workbench, the drying rack, the charge bench, the Field Slate board and every fast-travel terminus.

Three structural rules govern the whole map:

1. **Every zone is visited a minimum of twice** — once to survive it, once to *use* it once you have the capability that makes its second half legible. A zone visited only once has failed review.
2. **Every spoke opens a one-way shortcut back to Fold Camp on first completion.** The player walks *out* the hard way and comes *back* the cheap way, forever. No zone is ever traversed at full length twice.
3. **The lock is always a capability, never a key item.** There are no coloured keys on Vardholm. Doors open because Nadia now knows how to hear through them, climb them, power them, or read them. This is the anti-softlock foundation (see §5).

**Vertical structure.** The caldera is a bowl. Shore at 0 m, camp at 4 m, jungle climbing to 90 m, ruins in the rim wall at 120–180 m, mountain at 240 m, the volcano vent bowl at 160 m on the inner face, and the whole facility layer at −20 m to −140 m *beneath* all of it. The player's mental model should be: **I have walked the rim, now I go down through it.**

## 1. ZONE SPECIFICATIONS

### ZONE 1 — THE RIBCAGE *(Shore)*

**VISUAL IDENTITY**
Black volcanic sand, wet and mirror-flat at low tide so the whole beach doubles the sky. Palette is bruise: charcoal sand, oxide-orange rust, bone-white salt crust, one hard note of safety-orange from Nadia's dry bag. Light is overcast diffuse — the cloud lid kills all hard shadow, so form reads by silhouette and specular wet-sand sheen, never by shading. The signature landmark is the **six hulls**: wooden and steel wrecks from four different centuries, bows all pointing the same bearing, spaced at intervals that are *too regular* — a 1790s whaler's ribs, a rusted-out steam tender, a fibreglass trawler. They are a breakwater someone built out of other people's disasters. Sound bed: the surf is *wrong* — it arrives as a deep unbroken sigh rather than a crash, because the hull line has already taken the energy out of it. Gull absence is conspicuous. Under everything, a 0.4 Hz creak of loose plating.

**SCALE** · 320 m of beach arc × 60 m deep, plus three hull interiors. ~2,400 m² playable. **First visit: 45–55 min.**

**UNIQUE RESOURCES**
- **Marine-grade salvage cordage** — 8 mm braided polypropylene off the trawler's net drum. Only here because it's the only zone with 20th-century fishing gear; Fernmaw's plant fibre is weaker and wet-rots.
- **Copper sheathing plate** — hand-peeled from the 1790s hull's anti-fouling. Nowhere else on the island has soft, flat, workable metal. It becomes the Catchment's condensing surface and, later, Oleander gasket shim.
- **Dry driftwood above the tideline** — the only fuel that isn't saturated. Fernmaw's wood will not light.
- **Salt crust** — scraped from the upper berm pans. Used for food preservation and, critically, as the electrolyte in the Act 3 battery revival.
- **Intact 20 L plastic drums** — washed up. The only rigid watertight containers on Vardholm. Everything else is steel and rusted through.

**SIGNATURE PUZZLE — "The Wrong Wave"**
*Broken:* Nadia needs one litre of fresh water inside 36 hours and the Catchment condenses too slowly in still air. It needs airflow across the copper plate.
*Observe:* Standing between hull four and hull five, there is a permanent cold cross-draught — the gap between the hulls acts as a venturi, and the black sand there is dry when everywhere else is wet. The player must notice the dry patch, not be told. A gull feather held up (or the dry bag's loose strap) shows the airflow direction.
*Combine:* Copper plate + salvage cordage + a drum cut open with the multitool + the driftwood frame = **the Catchment**, sited in the venturi gap and oriented across the draught, not into it.
*World change:* The Catchment becomes a permanent, refilling water source at a fixed rate (0.4 L per in-game night) and, crucially, **teaches the game's entire thesis as a survival chore**: geometry plus a thermal gradient produces water. Four thousand years earlier, the Tolo Vardh did the same thing at the scale of a mountain. The Combs in Act 4 are this puzzle with three more zeroes on it, and the player should feel the recognition physically.

**STORY CLUES**
1. **The ketch's transom, 60 m offshore, visible at low tide only.** Nadia's own boat. Reveals the EPIRB housing is torn open and empty — establishing, physically rather than by narration, that no one is coming.
2. **A brass shipping tag wired to the trawler's net drum**, stamped `ORRIMOND TRUST — STN. VARDHOLM — 11.04.97 — H.L.` Reveals that someone was doing inventory on a wreck in 1997, twenty-one years after the last dated document anyone will find in Act 3. The player cannot interpret it yet. It is the first thread.
3. **The hull spacing itself, once the player has walked the full arc.** The Field Slate auto-logs a sketch; the intervals are 34 m, 34 m, 34 m, 34 m, 34 m. Reveals: this is a constructed structure, not a graveyard.

**DANGER**
Tidal cutoff and cold. The Ribcage's north third floods to chest height on the incoming tide, and the hull interiors flood completely. Nadia's core temperature drops in wet clothing under the cloud lid's permanent 14 °C. Mitigation is the **tide table** — the player reads the wrack line, and from Act 3 reads Lorvik's chalked tables, converting a hazard into a schedule. There is no drowning fail-state: cutoff strands the player in a hull until the tide turns, costing hours and warmth, not life.

**SECRET — The Seventh Hull**
A hundred and forty metres north, past a rock spur most players read as the zone boundary, is a seventh wreck: a small steel launch, *maintained*. Bilge pumped, hull patched with sheet copper, a mast step reinforced, and a hand-lettered waterline mark with dates going back to 2009. It is Hanne Lorvik's outbound supply drift — the boat from the Relief ending. Finding it in Act 1 makes Act 5 land harder and unlocks a Field Slate entry that can be *revised* four times across the game.

**LOCKS** · None. This is the start.

**SHORTCUT** · The **berm stair** — after the Catchment is built, Nadia cuts and stakes a switchback up the 9 m sand bluff to Fold Camp's south boardwalk, replacing a 400 m detour around the spur. One-way stake-and-rope descent becomes a two-way stair once the camp's timber is available in Act 3.

### ZONE 2 — FERNMAW *(Jungle)*

**VISUAL IDENTITY**
You are walking in a green throat. A collapsed stone aqueduct trench, 6–9 m wide, cut dead straight through the hillside four thousand years ago, now roofed over by tree-fern canopy that has grown up from inside the channel and closed overhead. Light is submarine: everything is filtered through three layers of frond, so the palette is a narrow band of oxidised greens — verdigris, olive, black-green shadow — punctuated by the **wet grey of dressed stone** wherever moss has been scraped off. And it *has* been scraped off, in a strip down the middle, because someone walks here weekly. The signature landmark is **the Split Aqueduct**: a 40 m section where the trench roof has failed and a single shaft of daylight falls through onto a stone floor still holding a shallow film of running water, with ferns leaning into the light from all sides like a crowd. Sound bed: continuous heavy drip on three timescales, insect stridulation that cuts out entirely when Nadia stops moving, and — the hook — a faint 11-minute rhythmic exhalation from a fissure in the trench wall that a first-time player will mistake for wind.

**SCALE** · 1.1 km of trench in three branches (main channel, a silted spur to the Combs' base, a service branch to the Sweatways mouth), plus 200 m of off-trench canopy floor. **First visit: 70–85 min.**

**UNIQUE RESOURCES**
- **Tree-fern rachis** — the stiff, straight, fibrous stem. Splits into strong flexible strips. The island's only source of *long* structural fibre; the Ribcage's polypropylene is short and salvage-limited.
- **Resin nodules** from cut fern crowns — the only natural waterproofing sealant. Needed for the dry box, later for gasket compound.
- **Charcoal-fungus (bracket growth on the trench walls)** — burns cool and smokeless. Required for any indoor fire and for the Act 3 filter media.
- **Standing potable film water** at the Split — 0.2 L/hour, free but fixed-location. Its existence is the first clue that Fernmaw is *plumbing*.
- **Cut-stone rubble with tool marks** — the only place the player can carry away a fist-sized sample of Tolo Vardh masonry for comparison later.

**SIGNATURE PUZZLE — "The Grade"**
*Broken:* The main trench is blocked at metre 600 by a fern-root plug and forty years of silt, and the water that should run this channel is going somewhere else. Beyond the plug is the only walkable route east to Fold Camp.
*Observe:* The trench floor is graded at a constant, gentle fall — not eroded, *cut*. Where the player can see clean floor they can see the grade continues through the plug. Above the plug, in the canopy, the machete cuts on the liana are pale and weeping sap: **days old.** Someone clears this and stopped partway. And 30 m back there is a stone **diversion sill** with a slot in it that is currently holding a slab of basalt on edge, sending the water into a side spur.
*Combine:* Fern rachis strips lashed with cordage into a lever + the copper plate as a digging blade + water. The sequence is not "chop the plug" — it is: lift the diversion slab out of the sill with the lever, let four decades of impounded side-spur water back into the main channel, and **let the water cut the silt out of the plug for you** while you deal with the roots. This is the game's first statement that you work *with* the ancient system's design intent, never against it.
*World change:* Water runs the length of Fernmaw permanently. The trench becomes a continuous walkable corridor with the game's first proper fast axis — and the running water audibly changes the zone's entire sound bed, which is the setup for every acoustic puzzle to follow. Downstream, water appears at Fold Camp's tidal flat and at the Combs' base, changing both zones before the player has seen them.

**STORY CLUES**
1. **A machete in a stump at the Split**, handle wrapped in modern electrical tape, blade sharpened so many times the profile is a thin crescent. Reveals: a tool maintained over decades by one person with one sharpening stone, and that they left it here expecting to come back.
2. **A galvanised lantern post at every 100 m of trench**, each with a brass tag: `L-04 · 14.02.24 · WICK+GLASS`. Reveals the maintenance-ledger grammar the whole game will run on — *dated objects as a navigation system*. Following ascending tag dates is a legitimate route-finding strategy from here on.
3. **Rev. Poulter's survey cairn**, a stacked-stone marker with a lead plaque: `CORMORANT TRUST · TRANSIT MARK IX · 1887 · BURIAL TERRACES BEAR 062°`. Reveals the 1883 misinterpretation in a single line — they thought the Combs were graves — and points a bearing the player will follow in Act 4.

**DANGER**
Humidity and footing. Nadia's field recorder and hydrophone are degrading — the game runs a visible **moisture meter on gear, not on Nadia**, which is the correct pressure for this fiction. Wet electronics fail intermittently before they fail permanently. Mitigation: the dry box (crafted from the drum, resin and charcoal-fungus desiccant), and routing through the Split's sunlight to dry out. Secondary danger: the trench walls are slick basalt at 1.5° and the silt sections are knee-deep; a fall costs gear condition, never a life. No fauna threat of any kind. Per Canon Rule 3, there is no creature in Fernmaw, and the insect stridulation cut-out is a *pressure-and-breeze* effect from the fissure, explained in Act 2.

**SECRET — The Sixth Register**
Sixty metres up the silted spur, behind a fern curtain, the trench wall carries a panel of notch-and-crescent marks — six vertical columns, each column a different carver's hand, each ending in the same closing mark. A player who photographs it in Act 1 and returns with **the Register** capability in Act 4 gets the game's quietest gut-punch: it is a six-generation handover roster, and column six ends with a closing mark that means *the work continues*. It is the Tolo Vardh equivalent of Lorvik's logbook, and it is four thousand years old.

**LOCKS** · Requires **fire and a cutting edge** (Ribcage Act 1) to enter past the first 80 m — the entry is choked with dead fern and there is no way through without a blade, and no reason to go without the Catchment proving water exists inland.

**SHORTCUT** · **The Sluice Ride.** Once The Grade is solved, the restored channel's lower 300 m has enough flow and fall that Nadia rides it down on a lashed fern-rachis sled to the Fold Camp boardwalk in 20 seconds. One-way, repeatable, and it is the single most *fun* traversal in the game. The return is the graded walk, which is fine because it is short and downhill-lit.

### ZONE 3 — FOLD CAMP *(Abandoned Camp — THE HUB)*

**VISUAL IDENTITY**
Eleven orange fibreglass huts on galvanised stilts, standing two metres above a grey tidal flat that floods and drains twice a day, so the camp is alternately an island and a shantytown on legs. The orange has chalked to a pale apricot on the sun side and stayed vivid on the shaded side — the huts are two colours depending on which way you walk past them. Palette: apricot and rust orange, flat pewter mudflat, bleached-bone white (the laundry, still on the line, three decades past clean), and a persistent wet-black where the flat never dries. Light is the island's most open — the cloud lid is thin over the breach — giving a high flat silver luminance with no sun disc. Signature landmark: **the boot line**. Every hut door has a pair of rubber boots outside it, paired, toes to the door, and they have been *re-paired* — the rubber has perished and been re-sorted by someone with a system. Sound bed: wind in stilt bracing at two pitches, the tidal flat's soft suck-and-tick as it drains, halyard slap from the dead mast, and — once the player restores power in Act 3 — a 50 Hz transformer hum that is the most comforting sound in the game.

**SCALE** · 180 × 140 m of flat, eleven huts (six enterable at first visit, all eleven by Act 4), a generator shed, a jetty, a boat shelter. **First visit: 60 min. Cumulative time across the game: ~4 hours.** This is the hub — it must support repeat visits without repeat exploration.

**HUB FUNCTIONS** *(the reason it is the centre and not a spoke)*
- **The Workbench** (Hut 3) — all crafting above field-tier. Field crafting stays possible anywhere; anything with tolerance happens here.
- **The Drying Rack & Dry Store** (Hut 7) — gear moisture reset. The only place moisture goes to zero.
- **The Charge Bench** (generator shed, Act 3+) — the field recorder, the lamp and the Reel Deck recharge here and nowhere else. This is the soft economy that makes the hub load-bearing: expedition length is bounded by battery, and battery lives at home.
- **The Slate Board** (Hut 1's wall) — the Field Slate's physical expression. Pinned clues, string, and the *revision* interface where earlier conclusions get crossed out and rewritten. Story beats land here, not in a menu.
- **Fast-travel terminus.** All five shortcuts arrive here. There is no fast travel *out* — only in. Outbound is always walked, which preserves the sense of expedition while removing the tedium of return.

**UNIQUE RESOURCES**
- **1970s consumable stock** — lamp oil, D-cells (dead, revivable), wire, fuses, hose clamps, PTFE tape. The only manufactured spares on Vardholm.
- **Fibreglass panel and marine ply** — hut cladding, cut for repairs. Only rigid sheet material on the island.
- **Diesel** — 40 L remaining in a buried tank, gelled and needing filtration. Finite by design and the only bulk fuel. Runs the generator.
- **Photographic chemistry and undeveloped film** (Hut 9, Nell Obuya's darkroom). Unique story resource — developing rolls is a minigame that yields evidence.
- **Potable water via the restored Fernmaw channel** — which only exists because the player solved The Grade. The hub is *made habitable by the player's own work*, which is why it feels like theirs.

**SIGNATURE PUZZLE — "Thirty-One Volts"**
*Broken:* The camp's generator is seized, the fuel is gelled, and the switchboard's main breaker will not hold. Without power there is no charge bench, no darkroom, no Reel Deck, and no Act 3.
*Observe:* Three separate observations the player must connect. (a) The generator's log card shows it was last run in 1989 — but the *battery* on the charge shelf has a brass tag dated four years ago, meaning someone has been maintaining a battery for a generator they never start. (b) The switchboard trips only when circuit 4 is closed, and circuit 4 runs out of camp on a cable that disappears into the mudflat heading north-east, toward Ash Throat. (c) The salt crust from the Ribcage, the distilled water from the Catchment, and a 1970s hydrometer in the store are exactly the kit for reviving a lead-acid cell.
*Combine:* Salt crust + Catchment water + hydrometer + the maintained battery = a cell with enough cranking amps for one attempt. Diesel + charcoal-fungus filter media + the copper plate as a funnel = fuel that will burn. Then the real answer: **isolate circuit 4 at the switchboard** rather than repairing it. The camp comes up.
*World change:* Light, warmth, the charge bench, and the darkroom. Fold Camp converts from a ruin to a home in one lever-pull, and this is the emotional midpoint of Act 3. The isolated circuit 4 is the hook into Ash Throat: a live 1970s cable running to a volcano is not a thing you leave alone.

**STORY CLUES**
1. **Ruben Castellar's load calculations** (Hut 5, graph paper, Portuguese and English, weighted under a rock). Reveals that his 1974 structural loads *cite datum surfaces that predate the camp by 3,900 years* — Wellhead were copying, not building. The exact fact: his safe working load table for "Cell 6 upper lip" assumes a stone section he did not cut and could not have measured without accepting it was engineered.
2. **The radio in Hut 2** — a 1970s HF set, dead since 1989, with **new alkaline batteries in the tray, corrosion-free, installed within the last two years.** Reveals: someone is still maintaining a radio they know is dead. It is not a communication device. It is a ritual.
3. **Tomas Ré's grave**, 60 m up the shore path — carved marker, dated 1989, and *fresh shore flowers with the stems cut square*. Reveals the caretaker rotation existed, that one caretaker died here, and that another one is tending him. This is the Act 3 gut-drop.
4. **The Orrimond mail tray** (Hut 1, only reachable in Act 4) — hundreds of stamped, addressed, unmailed cassette letters in Ré's hand, and on top of them one **unopened** 1994 envelope from the Orrimond Trust's solicitors. Reveals the dissolution — and that Lorvik chose not to read it.

**DANGER**
The tidal flat. Fold Camp floods to 1.4 m twice daily and the mud has soft patches that will hold a boot and take twenty minutes to escape. Mitigation: the **boardwalk network**, which the player extends with marine ply across the game, and Lorvik's chalked tide tables once found. Secondary: fibreglass dust and 1970s chemistry in Hut 9 — the darkroom needs ventilation opened before use, and a player who ignores it loses an hour to a headache, not a life. Third: diesel is finite. Burning it carelessly on lighting costs Act 4 capability. That is a resource-discipline pressure, not a death threat, and it is the only true scarcity economy in the game.

**SECRET — Hut 11**
The eleventh hut is not on the camp plan pinned in Hut 1 and its door is on the mudflat side, reachable only at low tide. It is Hanne Lorvik's. It is clean. There is a made bed, a shelf of books read to destruction, a chair facing a window that looks at the sea rather than the camp, and **the wall of unmailed letters — her own, not Ré's**, in handwriting that degrades across thirty-one years. She is not here. Finding Hut 11 in Act 3 rather than Act 5 reframes the entire back half: you have been in her house and she knows it.

**LOCKS** · Reachable at the end of Act 1 via either the Ribcage berm or the Fernmaw trench — **two independent routes in, by design**, because the hub can never be gated behind a single solve.

**SHORTCUT** · Fold Camp *is* the terminus. Its outbound contribution is the **north boardwalk**, extended in Act 3, which cuts the Ash Throat approach from a 25-minute mudflat slog to a 6-minute walk and remains passable at high tide.

### ZONE 4 — THE SWEATWAYS *(Caves)*

**VISUAL IDENTITY**
Lava tubes at blood heat, running with water. The tube walls are the natural glassy black-brown of basalt rope-flow, and set into them at intervals are **cut-stone baffles** — dressed slabs standing across the tube at deliberate angles, forcing the air to switchback. Palette: wet black, the warm ochre of mineral staining, and the surprising soft cream of the **ceramic drip-combs** — fired clay fins, hundreds of them, set into the ceiling in ranks, still shedding water after four thousand years. Light is entirely the player's: helmet lamp throws a hard cone into steam, and the steam makes every beam a solid object. The signature landmark is **the Bellows Gallery**, a 30 m chamber where nine baffles stand in a row like the leaves of a book, and where the eleven-minute pressure cycle is violent enough to visibly move the steam and press on the player's ears. Sound bed: this is the loudest zone. Broadband water roar, the baffles' tuned whistle rising and falling on the eleven-minute cycle, and a deep sub-bass that the player feels in the controller before they hear it.

**SCALE** · 900 m of tube in a rough figure-eight, four chambers, two flooded sections, 40 m of vertical. **First visit: 75–90 min. Second visit (Act 4, with Register): +40 min.**

**UNIQUE RESOURCES**
- **Ceramic comb fragments** — fired Tolo Vardh clay. Chemically inert, heat-proof, machinable with a file. The only material that survives the Ash Throat vent gases; every tool that goes into Zone 7 is tipped or lined with this.
- **Mineral-stained thermal water** — hot, non-potable, dissolved sulphates. Used for the darkroom's fixer bath and, in Act 4, as the Combs' test fluid because its mineral load makes flow paths visible.
- **Sulphur-free warm air** — the zone is the only place on Vardholm gear dries *fast*. A drying cache here extends expedition range enormously and is the reason players choose to route through the caves.
- **Intact baffle wedges** — small dressed stones, hand-sized, cut at exact repeated angles. Carrying one out and comparing it to Combs masonry is how the player proves the two zones are one system.

**SIGNATURE PUZZLE — "The Eleven-Minute Breath"**
*Broken:* The route east through the Bellows Gallery ends at a flooded siphon that is impassable — except it is not always flooded. The water level in the siphon rises and falls, and the player cannot predict it.
*Observe:* The hydrophone (acquired at the start of Act 2, making this the zone that *teaches the signature verb*) shows the gallery as a directional spectrogram. Aimed at the baffles, it resolves the whistle into nine discrete tones. Aimed at the floor, it shows water moving *behind* the rock. The cycle is eleven minutes; the siphon drains for **fifty seconds** at the cycle's low point, and the audible cue is the third baffle's tone dropping out — a note *disappearing* is the warning, not a note appearing. The player must learn to listen for absence, which is the thematic seed of the entire game.
*Combine:* Nothing is combined. This puzzle is deliberately craftless — it is the moment the game hands over its real verb and says *the tool is your attention*. Optional: a ceramic fragment wedged in baffle three flattens the tone and makes the window easier to hear, a genuine accessibility-as-diegesis solution.
*World change:* The east route opens permanently after the player braces the siphon's roof slab on the far side (fern rachis + baffle wedges), converting a timed 50-second window into an open passage — but the eleven-minute cycle *stays audible island-wide from this point*, and the player now knows what it is: the caldera breathing. Every subsequent zone's ambient mix carries it. They cannot un-hear it.

**STORY CLUES**
1. **A Sabo reel** in a dry alcove at the tube mouth, playable only with the Reel Deck in Act 3 — which means the player finds it, cannot use it, and must come back. *"Log twenty-two. The combs are not decorative and I will not be putting that in the quarterly. They are a heat exchanger and they are better than anything we have drawn."* Reveals Sabo understood the system a decade before Wellhead arrived, and that she chose not to report it.
2. **Solheim's bore holes** — three 80 mm diamond-cored holes punched clean through a baffle, with a 1974 chalked reference number beside each. Reveals the *mechanism of the damage*, physically, before any document explains it. The player will find the matching number in Solheim's day-book in Act 3 and the Field Slate will offer the revision.
3. **A caretaker's chalk mark** on the fourth baffle: a tide-height figure, a date from last month, and a tally. Reveals Lorvik measures this cycle routinely. She is not surviving here. She is taking readings.

**DANGER**
Heat, humidity and the cycle. Core temperature rises here — the inverse of the Ribcage — and Nadia's working time in the deep tubes is bounded at roughly 25 minutes before she must return to a cool draught. Mitigation: route planning against the mapped cool spots, and the ceramic-lined canteen. The flooded sections will not drown the player (the cycle always drains within eleven minutes) but will soak and stop all gear, costing a full return to Fold Camp's dry store. Rockfall is signposted by a specific dust-fall VFX two seconds ahead. No fauna.

**SECRET — The Tally Chamber**
A side tube most players walk past carries, on one wall, a continuous notch-and-crescent tally in six distinct carving hands — and on the opposite wall, in chalk, **Hanne Lorvik's tally, started in 1994, deliberately begun in the same grammar.** She taught herself their notation from the walls and joined the roster. Nobody asked her to. She just did not want the column to end.

**LOCKS** · The tube mouth is 4 m up a wet basalt face in Fernmaw's service branch, and there is no handhold. Opened by the **fern-rachis ladder** (Act 2 craft). Entry into the Bellows Gallery proper requires the **hydrophone**, without which the siphon is unsolvable and the game will not pretend otherwise — an NPC-free "you need to hear this" gate, communicated by Nadia's own caveat-laden narration.

**SHORTCUT** · **The Warm Chimney.** A 30 m vertical tube with Tolo Vardh cut footholds, exiting behind Fold Camp's generator shed. One-way down on the fern ladder rigged from above; becomes two-way once the player fixes a knotted line in Act 3. Its real value is that it turns the Sweatways into the *fast route* between camp and the Combs for the rest of the game.

### ZONE 5 — RIME SHOULDER *(Mountains)*

**VISUAL IDENTITY**
Above the cloud lid. The single most important shot in the game: the player climbs out of grey soup into blinding, absolute, unfiltered sunlight and a sky so saturated it reads as wrong after nine hours of overcast. Below, the cloud lid is a flat white floor from horizon to horizon, and Vardholm's summit is an island *in that*, not in the sea. Palette: bleached rock the colour of old bone, hard cobalt sky, blue-shadowed old snow in the lee gullies, and the one non-natural note — new **hemp rope**, pale gold, spliced over the corroded steel cable it replaced, on every bridge. Light is brutal and directional, the only hard shadows in the game. Signature landmark: **the Downed Mast** — a 1972 steel lattice HF antenna, collapsed across a ridge saddle, and comprehensively *stripped*: every cable cut cleanly, insulators removed and stacked, the baseplate bolts in a jar. It was not destroyed. It was decommissioned by someone tidy. Sound bed: **silence**. Real silence — the zone's defining feature is that the hydrophone shows a flat noise floor for the first and only time, and the wind is thin and high and carries nothing.

**SCALE** · 700 m of ridgeline, four bridge spans, three summit points, 160 m of vertical from the cloud lid. **First visit: 65 min.**

**UNIQUE RESOURCES**
- **Rime ice / clean meltwater** — the only zero-mineral water on Vardholm. Required for the darkroom and for the Oleander console's manometer fluid.
- **Stripped mast components** — copper feedline, ceramic standoff insulators, a rotator gearbox. The only high-voltage-rated parts on the island; the Act 5 transmitter is built from these and only these.
- **Dry hemp and lichen tinder** — desiccated by altitude. The only tinder that survives being carried through the Sweatways.
- **Line of sight** — not an item but a resource: this is the only place the field recorder can capture a clean, wideband, noise-floor-free reference recording, which is the *calibration* every later acoustic puzzle is measured against.

**SIGNATURE PUZZLE — "The Reference"**
*Broken:* Nadia wants to transmit. The mast is down and stripped, and even rebuilt it will not radiate — but that is not the puzzle, because the real problem is that she does not yet know *what she would transmit*. The actual break is that her hydrophone is uncalibrated: every reading she has taken in the caves and jungle is relative, and relative readings are exactly what ended her career.
*Observe:* From the summit the cloud lid is flat, but it is not featureless — directly over Ash Throat, 1.4 km away, the fumarole plume punches through it in an arc of vents that, from this height and no other, resolves into **a deliberate curve**. Nature does not lay out vents on a circular arc. Second observation: the mast's guy anchors are original 1972 but the *rope* on the nearest bridge is new — the player can see maintenance and abandonment side by side in one frame.
*Combine:* Field recorder + hydrophone + the summit's true noise floor = a **reference capture**. Then the ceramic standoff insulators + copper feedline + the rotator gearbox, carried down, become the Act 5 transmitter's front end.
*World change:* The Field Slate's acoustic readings retroactively become *absolute*. Every spectrogram the player has taken, in every zone, is recomputed and re-displayed with real numbers — and one of Nadia's earlier Fernmaw readings now resolves to the same signature she deleted eighteen months ago. This is the Act 2 revelation delivered as a **UI change rather than a cutscene**, and it is the best beat in the middle of the game. Additionally, the Ash Throat arc is now a mapped, navigable bearing.

**STORY CLUES**
1. **The relay hut** at the bridge-two anchor — a 1.5 × 2 m concrete box with a bunk, a log and three Sabo reels on a shelf. Reveals, on playback: *"Log forty-one. Ferrier is telling London we are listening. We are not listening. Marguerite built gates and Ferrier signs for them without reading what they do."* First mention of "the gates."
2. **The mast's decommissioning card**, wired to the baseplate: `ANTENNA STN 2 — STRUCK & STORED — 12.08.96 — H.L. — REASON: NO TRAFFIC TO SEND.` Reveals: someone chose to dismantle the island's only voice, deliberately, and wrote down why, and the reason is the saddest four words in the game.
3. **A Wellhead survey theodolite** still on its tripod, levelled, pointed at Ash Throat's arc, with a bearing written on masking tape. Reveals Wellhead saw the arc too, and were measuring it — and the bearing on the tape is the bearing to the Quiet Room, which nobody in 1974 knew existed.

**DANGER**
Cold, exposure and falls. Above the lid it is −4 °C in full sun with a 30 km/h ridge wind, and Nadia has jungle clothing. Working time is hard-bounded at ~35 minutes before hypothermia mechanics begin, mitigated by layering (Fold Camp's 1970s cold-weather stock), by the relay hut as a warm room, and by planning the route to bank shelter. The bridges are the fall risk: **the hemp is new, the steel cable under it is not**, and the player must test each span with the hydrophone (a tap on the cable reads as a bright clean ring or a dull dead thud — a corroded strand does not ring) before committing weight. A failed span costs gear dropped to the cloud lid, recoverable later in Fernmaw, never a death.

**SECRET — The Cairn Field**
Beyond summit three, out of sight of the route, is a field of forty-one small stacked-stone cairns in neat rows. Each has a flat slate with a date and a ship's name or, more often, a bearing and a time. They are the ships Lorvik has heard pass and could not contact, memorialised one by one on the only ground on Vardholm where you can see the horizon. It is the Quiet Room's chalk floor, rehearsed in stone, and finding it in Act 2 makes Act 5's reveal a recognition instead of an explanation.

**LOCKS** · Requires **the fern-rachis ladder and rope traversal** (Act 2) to get onto the ridge, plus **cold-weather layering** — and the layering is at Fold Camp, which means the player must reach the hub first. This is the structure's first true hub-dependency and it is deliberate: Rime Shoulder is the zone that proves the hub matters.

**SHORTCUT** · **The Scree Run.** A 180 m controlled slide down the lee gully, which deposits the player at the Sweatways' upper entrance, from which the Warm Chimney drops them at camp. Two zones bypassed in ninety seconds. One-way by nature — you cannot climb scree.

### ZONE 6 — ASH THROAT *(Volcano)*

**VISUAL IDENTITY**
A vent bowl 200 m across, floored in crusted sulphur the colour of powdered mustard and old bone, cracked into polygonal plates that crunch and give underfoot. Not erupting — *venting*: forty or fifty fumaroles exhaling white steam at a lazy angle, and where the steam meets the cold air at the bowl's rim it condenses into a permanent low haze at knee height, so the player wades through cloud. Palette: sulphur yellow, arsenic-pale green mineral crust, rust-red iron staining, and the shocking oxidised **verdigris-green of copper pipe** — hundreds of metres of it, 1960s, run down into the bowl in a neat organised bundle by someone who needed heat and was not romantic about it. Signature landmark: **the Heat Take** — a concrete plinth at the bowl's centre where thirty copper lines converge into a manifold with phenolic valve handles, half-buried in sulphur crust, still warm, still under pressure, still working after sixty years. Sound bed: fumarole hiss at a dozen pitches, the deep organ-note of the largest vent, sulphur crust ticking as it expands, and the steady tick of the copper manifold.

**SCALE** · 200 m bowl plus a 400 m rim approach and the Heat Take's service trench. **First visit: 55 min.**

**UNIQUE RESOURCES**
- **Sublimated sulphur crystal** — pure, scrapeable. The only oxidiser on the island; needed for the Act 4 battery electrolyte and for Nell Obuya's darkroom chemistry.
- **Native copper pipe and phenolic fittings** — 1960s, salvageable in metre lengths. Oleander's damper gates need replacement runs and this is the donor site.
- **Geothermal heat as a service** — the Heat Take, once repaired, is the power source that heats Oleander and keeps the damper console's hydraulics from gelling. This is a *world-state* resource, not an inventory one.
- **Vent-baked clay** — the sulphur bowl's margin clay, fired by ground heat, which is the only material that will take a mould for a replacement gasket.

**SIGNATURE PUZZLE — "The Arc"**
*Broken:* Circuit 4 from Fold Camp terminates here, at the Heat Take, and the manifold is dead — its pressure has been routed to the wrong branch for decades. Downstream, Substation Oleander is cold, and a cold Oleander cannot be entered because its stair well is choked with a plug of ice-cold condensate.
*Observe:* Two things. (a) The fumarole arc the player saw from Rime Shoulder is not natural and is not decorative — walking it with the hydrophone shows that every vent on the arc sits above a Tolo Vardh **thermal chimney**, and the ancients tapped the exact same heat by the exact same geometry three thousand nine hundred years before Ferrier's plumbers did. (b) The manifold's valve positions do not match the tags wired to them: someone reconfigured it in a hurry and never re-tagged, and the tags date to `1976`. Ruben Castellar, on his way to seal the Sump, shut the heat off behind him.
*Combine:* Ceramic comb fragments (Sweatways) to line the tool that must work in vent gas + native copper offcuts + vent-baked clay gaskets + the Fernmaw resin as thread sealant. The player re-makes two failed unions and then re-opens the valves **in the order the ancient chimneys establish**, not the order the 1960s tags suggest — the arc teaches the sequence, the tags lie, and reading the ruin over the machine is the puzzle's whole argument.
*World change:* Heat flows to Oleander. Over the following in-game hours (not instantly — this is a thermal system and it behaves its age) the condensate plug in the Oleander stair melts and drains, opening Act 4. Simultaneously the Combs' upper cells begin to fog, which the player can see from Fernmaw, and Fold Camp gets hot water. One valve sequence changes three zones.

**STORY CLUES**
1. **Castellar's tag bundle** — twenty brass valve tags cut off with wire cutters and left in a coffee tin, dated `03.76`. Reveals he deliberately removed the information, which means he expected someone to come and did not want them turning it back on.
2. **A Wellhead thermal survey sheet**, laminated, wired to the plinth. Reveals Solheim's core insight and her fatal error in one document: `GRADIENT LOCKED TO SITE GEOMETRY — ABSTRACTION NOT POSSIBLE — RECOMMEND FORCED APERTURE TO RAISE YIELD.` She understood the design could not be copied, and her response was to modify the original.
3. **A caretaker's brass tag on the manifold**, `HEAT TAKE — CHECKED, NOT RESTARTED — 09.03.19 — H.L.` Reveals Lorvik has stood here, understood it, and chose not to. The player will spend the rest of the game working out whether she was right.

**DANGER**
Gas and crust. Hydrogen sulphide pools in the bowl's low places — invisible, heavier than air, and signalled by the game's only true instrument-read hazard: a copper coupon on Nadia's strap **blackens** in concentration, giving a visual warning with a real chemical basis and no HUD meter. Mitigation: route on the high plates, work upwind, time visits to the afternoon katabatic drainage, and use the ceramic-filter mask built at the workbench. The sulphur crust also gives way over voids; the mitigation is the fern-rachis probe pole and reading plate colour (pale = thin). No fauna. Per Canon Rule 1, there is nothing supernatural about the bowl and the volcano never erupts at any point in the game.

**SECRET — The Copper Line to Nowhere**
One copper run leaves the manifold heading south-west, away from Oleander, and disappears into the rim. Followed to its end it terminates at a small stone-lined basin at the edge of the Quiet Room's outer wall — **and it is the only 1960s hardware anywhere near that chamber.** Ferrier's people found the acoustic focus and ran heat to it, and then someone stopped them, and the line was never commissioned. A player who traces it arrives at the Act 5 door from the outside, in Act 3, and can hear chalk being written on the other side of the stone.

**LOCKS** · The rim approach floods at high tide and the mudflat crossing is impassable without the **north boardwalk** (Act 3). The bowl itself requires the **ceramic-filter mask**, which requires Sweatways ceramic — so the caves must be done first. Two prerequisite zones, both organic.

**SHORTCUT** · **The Service Trench.** The 1960s pipe trench runs 300 m from the bowl rim straight to Fold Camp's north boardwalk, roofed in corrugated sheet, walkable once the player clears three collapses. One-way to begin (a fixed ladder is missing at the bowl end) and two-way after Act 4's timber.

### ZONE 7 — THE COMBS *(Ancient Ruins)*

**VISUAL IDENTITY**
A basalt cliff face 120 m high, terraced into a honeycomb: hundreds of hexagonal-mouthed cells, each 2–3 m across, in ranked tiers. From below, at the base of the cliff in Fernmaw's shadow, it reads as organic and overwhelming — a beehive, a wasp nest, something grown. From above, on the rim path, it resolves into a grid with routing between the cells, spurs, junctions and returns: **a circuit board cut into a mountain.** The vertigo of that reframing is the zone's entire reason for existing. Palette: basalt grey-black, four thousand years of pale mineral bloom around every wet mouth (and bone-dry chalk white around the dead ones, so the *pattern of failure is visible from a kilometre away*), and one obscenity — a **galvanised steel ladder, 1972, bolted straight through a carved register panel**, its fixings cracking the stone. Light: the cliff faces north-east and catches the only oblique light on the island for forty minutes at dawn, when every cell mouth casts a shadow and the whole face becomes legible. Sound bed: each live cell hums at its own pitch depending on its water load, so the face is a chord — and a third of it is silent.

**SCALE** · 400 m of cliff face, 60 m of climbable terrace, 22 enterable cells, the settling gallery behind. **First visit: 80 min. Second visit (Register + hydrophone, Act 4): +60 min.**

**UNIQUE RESOURCES**
- **Fin-cut condensate** — the purest, coldest water on Vardholm, from the live cells. Not a survival resource by this point in the game but a *diagnostic* one: its flow rate is the readout for the whole system's health.
- **Register stone** — carved notch-and-crescent panels, some loose, portable, and comparable. The Register mechanic's physical vocabulary lives here.
- **Dressed basalt wedges cut to seven repeating angles** — the ancient system's standard parts. Replacements for missing baffles anywhere on the island.
- **Cormorant Trust quarry spoil** — 1880s cut faces and abandoned phosphate sacks. The only place the player can see the 1883–91 damage as a *volume* rather than a claim.

**SIGNATURE PUZZLE — "One Cell Wet"**
*Broken:* Of twenty-two accessible cells, six run wet, five run intermittently, and eleven are dry. The player's goal is to restore exactly one dry cell to full flow — not to fix the Combs, which is far beyond one person, but to *prove they can be fixed*, which is what Act 5's choice needs.
*Observe:* The **Register** (acquired here, from the panel the 1972 ladder is bolted through) converts the carvings from decoration into schematic: a cell's panel states its intake, its fin count, its designed throughput and its downstream dependency. The hydrophone shows which upstream feeds are live. Reading both together, the player finds that cell 14 is dry not because cell 14 is broken but because cell 9 — three tiers up, which the Cormorant Trust quarried the lip off in 1887 — no longer holds head. The chain is four links long and the break is not where the symptom is.
*Combine:* Dressed basalt wedges (from quarry spoil, in the right one of the seven angles, which the Register tells you) to rebuild cell 9's lip + Sweatways ceramic combs to replace nine cracked fins in cell 14 + Fernmaw resin bedding + Ash Throat heat, already flowing, to establish the thermal gradient the fins need + mineral-stained thermal water poured in at the top as a visible tracer so the player can *watch the path fill*.
*World change:* Cell 14 runs. The face's chord gains a note — and the game plays that note, alone, held, for eight seconds. Cell 14's outflow feeds the settling gallery, which drains into the Oleander intake, which raises the damper console's ballast reservoir to operable level. Act 4's endgame is now mechanically possible. It also permanently changes Fernmaw's water volume and the Ribcage's outflow plume, both visible.

**STORY CLUES**
1. **The register panel behind the 1972 ladder** — the Register capability itself, and its content: a six-generation maintenance roster with the *Weir-Keeper* title mark, throughput figures, and a handover notation. Reveals that this was a job, held by named holders, for six hundred years, and that the Tolo Vardh were administrators as much as engineers.
2. **Rev. Poulter's journal**, Vol. III, in the Cormorant foreman's hut at the quarry face. *"The rock sweats at the cold hour, as though pitying us; I have forbidden the men to speak of it as a miracle."* Reveals that he observed the condensation system working perfectly, wrote it down beautifully, interpreted it as divine pathos, and then quarried it out. The entry is dated eleven days before the cell-9 lip came down.
3. **Nell Obuya's 1972 contact sheet**, in a tin nailed under a terrace lip, showing the Combs **running wet across the whole face** — sixteen frames of a working machine, annotated in her hand: *"F. says this is the water supply. M.S. says it is the water supply. Nobody will say what else it is."* Reveals the before-state, photographically, so the player can measure the loss.
4. **Solheim's bore log**, matching the Sweatways holes. Reveals the exact date and the exact justification for the forced apertures.

**DANGER**
Falls and structural failure. The terraces are 4,000 years old and the 1972 ladder is bolted into cracked stone; the player must learn to test a terrace lip before loading it (tap it, listen — a sound-based traversal check, consistent with the whole design). Wet cells are slick. There is a vertical fall risk of 60 m and it is mitigated by rope, by the Register's structural notation (some panels literally state a safe load), and by never climbing wet at dawn. Secondary: the dry cells hold dead air and a player who enters one without the mask gets an oxygen-starvation warning, not a death — Nadia backs out involuntarily.

**SECRET — The Unfinished Cell**
At the face's far north end, past the quarry spoil, is cell 23: **half-cut, tools still in it.** Bronze picks, a stone maul, a shaped fin blank on a work ledge, and a register panel begun and abandoned mid-glyph. The Tolo Vardh did not decline or vanish mysteriously — they were in the middle of a working day in roughly 1380 BCE and then they were not, and the game never explains why, because Nadia cannot know and the fiction does not lie to fill gaps. It is the most restrained thing in the game and it should stay that way.

**LOCKS** · The cliff base is reachable from Fernmaw in Act 1 but the terraces are not climbable — the first 12 m is sheer and the 1972 ladder's lower section is missing. Opened by **rope traversal + the Warm Chimney route** (which delivers the player to the terrace at tier 4, above the missing section — the Sweatways are the way in, which is why the caves must precede the ruins). Full comprehension requires the **Register**, which is found here, and the **hydrophone**, which is not.

**SHORTCUT** · **The Settling Gallery Drain.** Behind cell 14, once it runs, a maintenance drain 1.1 m in diameter descends 90 m at a walkable 20° and exits at the Sweatways' east chamber, four minutes from the Warm Chimney and camp. One-way (it is too steep and too wet to climb) and it is the artery of the whole back half of the game.

### ZONE 8 — SUBSTATION OLEANDER *(Underground Facility)*

**VISUAL IDENTITY**
The seam. A poured-concrete 1960s facility driven *into* a Tolo Vardh chamber, and the architects did not hide the join — there is a visible line, floor to ceiling, where board-marked grey concrete meets dressed basalt, with the stone's carved fins continuing behind the modern wall and disappearing into it. Palette: institutional concrete grey-green, the warm brass and Bakelite-black of phenolic dials, cream enamel, and the deep matte black of four-thousand-year-old stone. Light is the game's only *artificial, stable, generous* light: fluorescent tubes behind wire cages, warm and humming, with the extraordinary effect that after eleven hours of overcast and lamplight the player finds the safest-feeling room in the game a hundred and forty metres underground. Signature landmark: **the damper console** — a 4 m curved desk of phenolic panels with four vertical gate columns, each with a ballast gauge, a vent lever and a tension wheel, and above them a mechanical tide-table drum that still turns. Sound bed: 50 Hz transformer hum, water moving in pipes behind the walls, relay clicks on the eleven-minute cycle, and — the reveal — when the player kills the fans for the first time, the **sound of the stone fins dripping**, which has been there the whole time under the machinery.

**SCALE** · Three levels — upper gallery, console hall, and the flooded Sump below — totalling ~2,800 m² across 14 rooms. **First visit: 100–120 min.** The game's densest zone.

**UNIQUE RESOURCES**
- **Ferrier–Oleander document archive** — the complete paper record. Not a crafting resource; the game's largest single deposit of truth.
- **Instrument-grade spares** — relays, gland nuts, phenolic panel stock, Sabo's own labelled parts bins. The only place a precision repair is possible.
- **The reel-to-reel library** — 60+ tapes, catalogued by Sabo, playable on the Reel Deck. Unique because it is the only *archive with an index*, which turns the player from a scavenger into a researcher.
- **Manometer fluid and calibration standards** — required to trust the console's readings, sourced with Rime Shoulder's clean meltwater.

**SIGNATURE PUZZLE — "Four Gates Against the Tide"**
*Broken:* Sabo's four damper gates are the modern prosthesis holding the caldera's resonance down, and they are drifting out of tune. Gate 3 has a failed gland (Sabo's own log says she fixed it twice), gate 1's ballast reservoir is below operable level, and the tide-table drum is running eleven days behind because nobody wound it.
*Observe:* This is the game's thesis expressed as a control surface. The console does not tell the player what to do. It gives them four ballast levels, four vent positions, four tension settings and a tide drum, and the hydrophone shows the **result in the caldera** in real time as a resonance node map. The player must observe that the correct setting is not static: it is a function of tide state, which changes hourly, which is why this job cannot be automated and why a human has to be here forever. The Register's Tolo Vardh notation gives the target damping values; Sabo's log gives the modern gates' response curves; the tide drum gives the schedule. Three documents, one console.
*Combine:* Vent-baked clay gasket + copper shim (Ribcage) + Sweatways ceramic packing to rebuild gate 3's gland. Cell 14's restored outflow (Combs) fills gate 1's ballast. Rime Shoulder meltwater recalibrates the manometers. **Every prior zone contributes exactly one component**, and the player feels the whole island converge on one desk.
*World change:* The player brings the resonance node map into balance for the first time in decades — and the island goes quiet. Every zone's ambient bed drops the sub-bass out. The eleven-minute cycle smooths. And in the silence, on the hydrophone, from one bearing, the player hears **a human voice, faint, counting.** Lorvik is on the other side of the Sump, and she has just heard the machine come right for the first time since 1976, which is why she finally stops hiding. This is the transition into Act 5 and it is delivered entirely through audio.

**STORY CLUES**
1. **Sabo's complete log set.** *"Log fourteen. Ferrier's 'anomaly' is a loose gland nut on Line Three. I have now fixed it twice."* And later: *"The gates are not instrumentation. They are a splint. I have told him in writing. He has filed it."* Reveals that the truth was known, documented, and administratively buried in 1968.
2. **The Sump door, sealed from inside**, with Castellar's chalk on the concrete: a date, `03.76`, and a torque figure for the dogs. Reveals that the Sump was sealed *by* a man who was inside it, deliberately, to stop the others making things worse — and that he did the calculation first.
3. **Solheim's final variance report**, unsigned. Reveals, in cost-benefit prose, that Wellhead modelled the harm from forced apertures, priced it, and proceeded. This is the institutional horror the bible calls for, stated in the register of a funding document rather than a confession.
4. **The Orrimond Trust operations manual**, 1976, which specifies the caretaker rotation as three-year terms with mandatory relief — a document that proves Lorvik's thirty-one years is not duty but *administrative abandonment*.

**DANGER**
Confined space, water and electricity. The Sump level floods on a cycle and the pumps are 1960s; a player who ignores the level gauge loses equipment and a long walk, never a life. The switchgear is live, unguarded and 1960s — the mitigation is procedural (isolate, tag, test, work), which the game teaches as a genuine safe-isolation ritual and which is far more tense than any monster. Confined-air pockets in the lower galleries require the mask. No fauna, no threat, no chase.

**SECRET — Sabo's Locker**
A steel locker in the plant room, not in the inventory, opened with a combination that the player can derive from a date she references sarcastically in three separate logs. Inside: her own resignation letter, written in 1969, **never submitted**; a tin of the gland packing she cut herself because the supplied stock was rubbish; and a hand-drawn schematic of a fifth gate she designed and never got funded, which would have made the system stable without a human. The tragedy of the entire game in one locker: the fix existed, on paper, in 1969, and nobody signed the purchase order.

**LOCKS** · The stair well is plugged with condensate ice until **Ash Throat's heat** is restored (Act 3 → Act 4 gate). The console hall requires **the Register** (Combs) to interpret and **the Reel Deck** (Fold Camp) to hear Sabo. The Sump stays sealed until the player has balanced the gates.

**SHORTCUT** · **The Cable Riser.** A 1960s vertical cable shaft with a fixed steel ladder runs from the upper gallery straight up 90 m and exits inside Fold Camp's generator shed — the shed the player has been standing in since Act 3, whose floor hatch they never questioned. Two-way immediately (it is a proper ladder) and it collapses the game's largest distance to twelve seconds. Revealing it should feel like the map folding in half.

### ZONE 9 — THE QUIET ROOM *(Final Area)*

**VISUAL IDENTITY**
A perfect circular chamber, 18 m across, at the caldera's geometric acoustic focus. The ceiling is carved into inverted fins — hundreds of them, radiating, converging on a point above the centre — and they are **wet**, four thousand years on, shedding a slow ring of drips onto a stone gutter at the wall. The floor is chalk. Not chalked: *chalk*, layer on layer on layer, thirty-one years of it, a palimpsest of dates, bearings, times and ship names spiralling out from the centre, the oldest at the middle worn to a grey smear by feet, the newest at the edge still sharp. Palette: chalk-white on wet black stone, one warm point of lantern light, and Lorvik's blue oilskin — the only saturated colour. Light is a single hurricane lamp on the floor, so the whole chamber is lit from below and every ceiling fin throws its shadow upward. Sound bed: **this is the quietest place on Vardholm and the most acoustically alive.** Footsteps arrive back from the ceiling a half-second late. A whisper at the centre is audible at the wall and inaudible two metres away. Water drips in a rhythm. And when two people talk here, their voices come back to them, which is exactly the right room for this conversation.

**SCALE** · One chamber, 18 m diameter, plus a 90 m approach gallery. **First visit: 40–50 min, and it is all conversation and reading the floor.**

**UNIQUE RESOURCES** · **None, deliberately.** There is nothing to pick up in the Quiet Room. Per the bible's Act 5 mandate, **the crafting HUD is stripped entirely on entry** and the only verbs are walk, listen and speak. Protect this in design review; every playtest will produce a note asking for one more craft here and the answer is no.

**SIGNATURE PUZZLE — "The Floor"**
*Broken:* Nothing is broken. This is the one zone with no mechanism to repair, and that absence is the design statement.
*Observe:* The floor. The player walks the spiral from the outer edge inward, reading it, and the chalk resolves into a record: a date, a bearing, a time, a vessel class where she could identify it, and — for the ones she could not — a blank. Thirty-one years of ships passing, heard on the hydrophone, uncontactable because the mast was struck in 1996 because there was no traffic to send. The oldest entries at the centre are dense and detailed. The recent ones are shorter. The last one is from six days ago and it reads, in full, a bearing and the word `KETCH` — **it is Nadia's own arrival**, logged, before the wreck, from inside this room.
*Combine:* Nothing. The player brings only what they have understood.
*World change:* Lorvik speaks. The conversation is the final mechanic: a long dialogue in which Nadia can only offer what she has actually found — the Field Slate's *revised* entries are the dialogue options, and an entry she logged and never revised produces a wrong, confidently-stated line that Lorvik quietly corrects. A player who rushed will be wrong in front of a woman who has been right and alone for thirty-one years. Then the choice: **THE RECORD** or **THE RELIEF**.

**STORY CLUES**
1. **The chalk floor itself** — as above. The single most important artifact in the game and it is a floor.
2. **The unopened 1994 Orrimond envelope**, which Lorvik produces from her oilskin. She has carried it for thirty-one years. She knows what it says. She explains why she never opened it, and the explanation is the game's thesis: an unopened letter is not an instruction, and as long as it is not an instruction she is still employed, and as long as she is still employed the work is legitimate rather than madness.
3. **The 11,000th logbook entry**, which she is writing when Nadia enters. *"Day 11,4— "* and then she stops, because for the first time since 1994 the next word is not `No vessel.`

**DANGER**
None, physically, and that is deliberate: the zone with the highest emotional stakes has zero mechanical threat, which is the inverse of every survival game's instinct and is correct for this one. The only risk is being wrong.

**SECRET — The Centre Mark**
Under thirty-one years of chalk, at the precise focus, the floor carries a Tolo Vardh **Weir-Keeper notch-and-crescent** — cut into the stone, worn smooth, with six strike-throughs. A player who has found the Fernmaw Sixth Register and the Sweatways Tally Chamber can, with the Register capability, read it: it is the same handover roster, and it is the *original*. Lorvik has been standing on it, chalking over it, for three decades, and she has no idea it is there. Showing it to her is an optional beat that changes three lines of the ending conversation in both branches. It is not required and most players will not find it, which is correct.

**LOCKS** · Opened only after the gates are balanced (Oleander, Act 4), because until the island goes quiet Lorvik will not unbar the approach gallery. She has been avoiding Nadia for eleven days and the door is shut from her side. This is the only lock in the game opened by another person.

**SHORTCUT** · The approach gallery becomes two-way and joins the Cable Riser at Oleander's upper level, meaning that after Act 5 begins, Quiet Room → Fold Camp is ninety seconds. Both endings need the player to be able to move between Lorvik and the transmitter, repeatedly, while deciding.

## 4. CRITICAL PATH — 25 BEATS

1. **Wake on the Ribcage**, face-down in black sand at low tide, dry bag still clipped to her wrist. Inventory: hydrophone (soaked), recorder (9%), multitool, pencil, Field Slate. Tomas Weir is not on the beach and shouting does not help.
2. **Walk the hull arc.** Salvage cordage from the trawler net drum, copper sheathing from the 1790s wreck, driftwood above the tideline. The Slate auto-sketches the hull spacing: 34 m, five times.
3. **Find the venturi** between hulls four and five — dry sand where everything else is wet. **Build the Catchment.** First litre at dawn. *Act 1 water goal met.*
4. **Find the ketch's transom** at low tide. EPIRB housing torn open and empty. Nadia writes the sentence that starts the game properly: nobody filed this trip, and this island is a shoal.
5. **Cut into Fernmaw** with a blade and a torch. Eighty metres in, she scrapes moss off the floor and finds dressed stone, graded, at a constant 1.5°. Above her, machete cuts on the liana are weeping sap. *Act 1 revelation.*
6. **Solve The Grade** — lift the diversion slab, let impounded water cut the silt plug. Water runs the length of Fernmaw for the first time since the 1970s. The zone's sound bed changes permanently.
7. **Ride the Sluice** down to **Fold Camp.** Eleven orange huts on stilts over a tidal flat, boots paired at every door, laundry three decades on the line. *Act 1 ends. Act 2 begins.*
8. **Dry out the recorder and hydrophone** in the Sweatways' warm air after building the dry box — and get the hydrophone working. **The spectrogram overlay comes online.** The world acquires its second layer.
9. **Aim the hydrophone at the Fernmaw fissure.** Direction and depth. The 11-minute cycle now has an address, and it is underground.
10. **Fern-rachis ladder** into the Sweatways. Heat, steam, cut-stone baffles, ceramic drip-combs still working after four thousand years. Find a Sabo reel she cannot yet play.
11. **The Eleven-Minute Breath.** Learn to listen for the *absence* of baffle three's tone. Cross the siphon in the fifty-second window, brace the roof slab, open the east route. Find Solheim's three bore holes and note them without understanding them.
12. **Warm Chimney** to camp. Fit cold-weather layers from the 1970s stock. **Climb to Rime Shoulder** via Fernmaw's canopy and the rope traverse, testing every span's cable by its ring.
13. **Break the cloud lid.** Sun, sky, silence, a white floor to the horizon. Take **the Reference capture** — and watch every prior reading in the Slate turn absolute. One Fernmaw capture matches the signature she deleted eighteen months ago. *Act 2 revelation.*
14. **The relay hut and the downed mast.** Sabo's first mention of "the gates." The decommissioning card: `STRUCK & STORED — 12.08.96 — H.L. — REASON: NO TRAFFIC TO SEND.` And from the summit, the fumarole arc resolves into a curve. Strip the mast for parts.
15. **Scree Run** down to the Sweatways and home. *Act 2 ends. Act 3 begins.*
16. **Thirty-One Volts.** Revive the battery with salt crust and Catchment water, filter the gelled diesel, isolate circuit 4, start the generator. **Fold Camp comes up.** Charge bench, darkroom, Reel Deck.
17. **Play everything.** Sabo on the combs as a heat exchanger. Castellar's load calcs citing surfaces 3,900 years older than his camp. Develop Nell Obuya's rolls. Learn the brass-tag ledger and realise the tags make a weekly round.
18. **Follow the freshest tags** to Tomas Ré's grave — fresh flowers, stems cut square — and to the Hut 2 radio, dead since 1989, with new batteries in it. *Act 3 gut-drop: someone is here, now.*
19. **North boardwalk to Ash Throat.** Mask on, copper coupon on the strap, work the high plates. Find Castellar's cut valve tags in a coffee tin and Solheim's `FORCED APERTURE` recommendation wired to the plinth.
20. **The Arc.** Re-gasket two unions and open the valves in the ancient chimneys' sequence, not the 1976 tags' order. Heat flows. Over the next in-game hours the Oleander stair's condensate plug melts, the Combs' upper cells begin to fog, and camp gets hot water. *Act 3 ends. Act 4 begins.*
21. **Warm Chimney to the Combs' tier 4.** Prise the 1972 ladder off the carved panel it was bolted through and **read the Register**: a six-generation maintenance roster, the Weir-Keeper title, throughput figures. Poulter's journal in the quarry hut. Nell's 1972 contact sheet of the whole face running wet. *Act 4 revelation.*
22. **One Cell Wet.** Trace cell 14's failure four links upstream to the lip the Cormorant Trust quarried off cell 9 in 1887. Rebuild the lip with correctly-angled wedges, replace nine fins with Sweatways ceramic, run mineral water as a tracer, and watch the path fill. The face's chord gains a note.
23. **Settling Gallery Drain** to the Sweatways, Warm Chimney to camp, then **down the generator shed's floor hatch** — the Cable Riser — into **Substation Oleander.** Concrete meeting dressed basalt on a visible seam. Sabo's archive. The Sump, dogged shut from inside, with Castellar's chalk and a torque figure. Sabo's locker and the fifth gate that was never funded.
24. **Four Gates Against the Tide.** Rebuild gate 3's gland, fill gate 1's ballast from cell 14, recalibrate with Rime Shoulder meltwater, wind the tide drum, and tune four gates live against the tide. **The island goes quiet.** In the silence, on one bearing, a human voice, counting. *Act 4 ends. Act 5 begins.*
25. **The Quiet Room.** She has unbarred the gallery. Crafting HUD gone; walk, listen, speak. Read the chalk floor inward — thirty-one years of ships that passed, ending six days ago with a bearing and the word `KETCH`. The unopened 1994 envelope comes out of her oilskin. **THE RECORD or THE RELIEF.** Both close on the ceiling fins, still dripping: faltering out of rhythm, or settling into one.

No segment exceeds 35 minutes without a save-anywhere-safe point. *(This is a design target, not a measured result — it needs validation in the first playable.)*

## 6. THE ANTI-SOFTLOCK RULE SET

These are engineering constraints, not guidelines. Each is testable and belongs in CI as a content-validation pass over the level data.

**R1 — No consumable is ever required twice, and no required consumable is finite.**
Every crafting recipe on the critical path draws from a node with a respawn rule: Fernmaw rachis and resin regrow on a 3-day in-game cycle, Ribcage driftwood and cordage restock on every spring tide, Sweatways ceramic fragments are effectively infinite (a field of thousands), Combs basalt wedges likewise. **Diesel is the single deliberate exception** — and therefore diesel is never required by any critical-path solve. It buys comfort (lighting, hot water, the jetty crane) and nothing else. *Validation pass: assert no node on the critical-path recipe graph has `finite: true`.*

**R2 — No one-way transition exists before every item needed on the far side is obtainable on the far side.**
All five shortcuts point *toward* the hub, which by construction is the zone with the full workbench, so "shortcutting yourself into a corner" is structurally impossible. The two genuinely one-way passages that do not terminate at the hub — the Scree Run and the Settling Gallery Drain — both discharge into the Sweatways, which has the Warm Chimney to camp. *Validation pass: assert every node in the traversal graph has a path to Fold Camp with zero item requirements.*

**R3 — Every gated zone has its key obtainable outside itself, or the key is inside and the zone is re-enterable.**
The Register is found inside the Combs, which is legal because the Combs never lock behind you. The hydrophone is repaired at the hub, not in the Sweatways it opens. *Validation pass: for every lock L on edge (A→B), assert the capability granting L is reachable without traversing (A→B).*

**R4 — No puzzle state is destructible.**
Every solve is reversible or idempotent. If the player pulls the Fernmaw diversion slab and drops it in the water, a replacement slab spawns at the sill. The Ash Throat valve sequence can be re-run from any position. Gate settings on the Oleander console can be moved freely and the "solved" check is a tolerance band, not a sequence — the player cannot lock the console into a wrong state. *Validation pass: every puzzle object declares `reversible: true` or `respawns: true`.*

**R5 — Timed windows never gate progress permanently, and there are no fail states on timers.**
The Sweatways' fifty-second siphon window repeats every eleven minutes, forever. Tides repeat every 6h12m. Rime Shoulder's hypothermia timer ends in forced retreat to the relay hut, not death. Ash Throat's H₂S pockets push the player out of the bowl, not into a game-over. **There is no death in this game** — the failure verbs are *soaked*, *cold*, *stranded*, *dropped*, each costing time and gear condition. *Validation pass: assert zero `PlayerDeath` transitions exist in the state machine.*

**R6 — Gear is damageable but never destroyable, and never loseable.**
The hydrophone, recorder, Reel Deck, Field Slate and multitool cannot be dropped, broken beyond repair or lost. Dropped items always land in a recoverable place (Rime Shoulder drops land in Fernmaw's canopy with a Slate marker; Sump drops surface at the settling gallery on the next cycle). Water damage is a *condition*, cleared at the dry store, never a permanent loss. *Validation pass: assert every item flagged `criticalPath: true` also has `destructible: false, droppable: false`.*

**R7 — The Field Slate is never wiped, and its objective layer is always populated.**
Per Canon Rule 7, the record is never destroyed by the plot. Mechanically this means the Slate always holds at least one actionable, non-stale objective with a map bearing — and if the player has genuinely exhausted every lead in the current act, the Slate degrades gracefully to a "re-walk the freshest brass tags" hint, which is always valid because Lorvik's round is always current. *Validation pass: run the critical-path bot to every reachable world-state and assert `activeObjectives.count > 0` at every node.*

**R8 — Two independent routes into the hub, always.**
The Ribcage berm stair and the Fernmaw trench both reach Fold Camp and neither can be closed. A single geometry bug, a stuck physics prop or a failed streaming load on one route cannot isolate the player from the workbench. This is the one piece of redundancy worth the content cost.

**R9 — Saves are non-destructive and multi-slot, with a rolling autosave ring.**
Autosave at every zone transition, every shortcut unlock and every puzzle solve, into a ring of five, plus three manual slots. No single-slot overwrite. Because there is no death, saves exist to protect against *content* faults, not player faults — so the ring must reach back across at least one full zone.

**R10 — Every critical-path solve is achievable with knowledge the player can obtain in-world, and stated twice in different media.**
Every one: once as a physical observation in the environment, once in a document, recording or carving. The Ash Throat valve order is in the fumarole arc's chimney sequence *and* in the register marks on the chimney heads. The Sweatways window is audible *and* written in Lorvik's chalk tally. A player who hates reading and a player who hates listening must both be able to finish. *Validation pass: every critical-path solve declares two `hintSources` with distinct `medium` values.*

## 7. FLAGGED ASSUMPTIONS

These are design claims that require verification before they are treated as settled. I have not verified them and they should not be repeated as fact.

- **Session-length and segment-timing targets in §5** are design intent derived from the structure, not measured playtest data. They require validation in the first playable.
- **60 fps on iPhone 12 / mid-range Android for the Combs' cliff face and Oleander's 14-room interior** is an assumption about achievable draw-call and memory budgets on Unity 6 URP that has not been prototyped. The Combs (hundreds of cell mouths, wet surfaces, a per-cell audio source) and Oleander (dense interior, many light probes, 60+ interactable props) are the two zones most likely to fail the budget; both need a vertical-slice performance test before art production. Per-cell audio in the Combs in particular is a likely early cut to a baked composite bed.
- **The acoustic premises** — a caldera acting as a re-radiating lens into the deep sound channel, passive condensation at the scale the Combs imply, and the hydrophone's directional-spectrogram behaviour — are invented fiction per Canon Rule 10. Nothing in this document should be read as a claim about real acoustics or hydrology, and an acoustics and a hydrology consultant must review before any public realism claim.
- **All proper nouns introduced or extended here** — the Bellows Gallery, the Heat Take, the Split Aqueduct, the Warm Chimney, the Sluice Ride, the Cable Riser, the Settling Gallery Drain, the Tally Chamber, the Cairn Field, the Seventh Hull, Hut 11, the Centre Mark — inherit the bible's **unverified trademark/title-clearance status** and must go through the same clearance pass.
